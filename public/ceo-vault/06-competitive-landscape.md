# Competitive Landscape

## Direct Competitors
| Competitor | Strength | Weakness | FORTIS Advantage |
|------------|----------|----------|------------------|
| MOSIP | Open-source, trusted | Toolkit only, no payments layer | Built for Gambia, payments integrated, commercial BOT model |
| Huawei | Scale, infrastructure | Geopolitical risk, donor resistance, vendor lock-in | Neutral, open standards, BOT transfer |
| Veridas | Biometric quality | Expensive, foreign-owned | Local ownership, sovereign data |
| Local mobile money (QMoney, Afrimoney) | Adoption, trust | Siloed, no interoperability | Connects not competes — we build the rails |
| MTN/Orange (WAEMU) | Regional scale | Operator-controlled, revenue extraction | Sovereign, government-owned after Year 7 |

## Positioning Statement
"FORTIS OS does not compete with local fintech. FORTIS OS enables local fintech. We build the rails. They build the trains. Everyone wins."

## Competitive Response Scripts

**If Huawei offers counter-proposal:**
"We respect Huawei's capabilities. Our donors prefer open-source, neutral technology. We use open standards. We can partner on hardware infrastructure while maintaining open-source software. But ownership must remain sovereign."

**If MOSIP bids directly:**
"MOSIP is a toolkit, not a solution. We use MOSIP as our identity core component. We add payments, data exchange, and commercial BOT model. MOSIP cannot deploy itself — we operationalize it."

**If 'do nothing' is proposed:**
"The cost of doing nothing is D420M annually in digital leakage and fragmentation. That is approximately 15% of the national budget. The question is not whether The Gambia can afford FORTIS OS. It is whether The Gambia can afford not to have it."

**If a local IT company bids:**
"We welcome local partnership. FORTIS OS is designed with local capacity building embedded. We will employ and train Gambian engineers. The system transfers to Gambian ownership at Year 7."
