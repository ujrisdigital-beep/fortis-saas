# Frequently Asked Questions — CEO Edition

**Q1: Why The Gambia first?**
The Gambia is the ideal proving ground: population of 2.5M (manageable scale), English-speaking, politically stable, existing mobile money infrastructure (QMoney, Afrimoney), AfCFTA signatory, and diaspora remittance economy. Success here creates a replicable model for West Africa's 400M people.

**Q2: Why not just use MOSIP or build on M-Pesa?**
MOSIP is a toolkit, not a deployed solution. It requires configuration, integration, and operational management — that is what FORTIS OS provides. M-Pesa is Kenya-owned and does not include identity or data exchange layers. FORTIS OS is the integration layer that connects identity, payments, and data exchange into one sovereign system.

**Q3: What if the next President cancels the project?**
FORTIS OS is structured as a national asset, not a partisan project. By Year 3, the system processes civil servant salaries, social payments, and business licenses. Cancellation would disrupt government operations — the political cost is prohibitive. We also pursue enabling legislation and international donor oversight to create legal and diplomatic protection.

**Q4: How do you compete with free products?**
Free products (WhatsApp, Google, Meta) monetize citizen data. FORTIS OS charges transparent transaction fees (0.5–1.5%) and does not monetize data. The value proposition is not price — it is sovereignty. Citizens and governments pay for ownership and control of their own data.

**Q5: What if the pilot fails technically?**
The BOT model protects the government: they pay nothing for non-performance. Our technical architecture uses open-source components (MOSIP) with global deployment records. We have contingency plans (offline mode, backup connectivity). A partial pilot success still generates learning that donors value.

**Q6: Is FORTIS OS profitable?**
Year 5 projected revenue: D300M (~$6.5M USD). Operating margins: ~45%. The system reaches break-even at 10M transactions per year — achievable by Year 3. From Year 3 onwards, FORTIS OS is sustainably profitable without donor dependency.

**Q7: What is the exit strategy?**
FORTIS OS does not exit. The BOT model transfers ownership to government at Year 7. FORTIS INVICTA continues as the preferred support and innovation contractor. Revenue continues through support contracts, new module development, and regional expansion.

**Q8: How is this different from previous failed projects?**
Previous failures (e.g., National ID projects in Gambia 2009, 2014) failed because: (1) no presidential champion, (2) no commercial model, (3) no interoperability design, (4) single-vendor dependency, (5) no BOT transfer mechanism. FORTIS OS addresses all five failure modes.

**Q9: How do you protect against surveillance misuse?**
Zero-knowledge proof architecture — verification is possible without revealing underlying data. Granular consent — citizens can see, limit, and revoke all data access. Immutable audit logs — all access is recorded and reviewable. No access without explicit, revocable consent. Parliamentary oversight committee can access all access logs.

**Q10: What happens to current systems (NaYA, APRC registry)?**
FORTIS OS is designed to integrate with existing systems, not replace them. We provide an API gateway that connects existing registries. Legacy systems continue to operate; we add interoperability. Over time, legacy systems upgrade via standard API integration.
