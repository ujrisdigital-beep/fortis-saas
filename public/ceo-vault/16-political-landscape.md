## Potential Opposition
| Opponent | Objection | Counter-Argument |
|----------|-----------|------------------|
| Local fintech (QMoney, Afrimoney) | "You will compete with us." | "We build the rails. You build the trains. Your addressable market triples." |
| Foreign technology vendors | "We can do this cheaper." | "Your model is rent extraction. Our model is transfer of ownership. These are fundamentally different." |
| Civil service bureaucracy | "We already have systems." | "Your systems are silos. We connect them. You become more valuable, not less." |
| Privacy advocates & civil society | "This is surveillance infrastructure." | "We publish our code. We invite independent audit. No access without consent. This is the opposite of surveillance." |
| Opposition politicians | "This is a government contract for political friends." | "BOT agreement is competitive. All contracts are public. International donors require transparent procurement." |

## Cross-Party Framing
FORTIS OS must be framed as a **national asset, not a government project**:
- "This infrastructure will serve whoever wins the next election"
- "The data belongs to citizens, not to any government"
- "We transfer ownership — no government can 'own' this to exclude another"
- "International donors require cross-party commitment as a funding condition"

## Political Transition Protocol
If administration changes:
1. System embedded in Ministry of Finance (salary payments) — critical function
2. Cross-party steering committee provides continuity
3. Enabling legislation makes cancellation require parliamentary vote
4. International donor agreements include continuity clauses
5. Public awareness campaign: "Your digital identity belongs to you, not to any government"
6. International arbitration clause protects BOT agreement across governments
